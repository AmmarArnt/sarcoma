## Patient Regimen Assessment — Food Sources Perspective

The patient is consuming fresh juices of celery, ginger, carrot, broccoli, and apple during chemo rest weeks. The preparation method (juicing) has a critical consequence for one compound:

**Broccoli juice and sulforaphane**: Centrifugal or high-speed juicing destroys myrosinase through mechanical shear and oxidative inactivation. Without myrosinase, glucoraphanin cannot be converted to sulforaphane in the plant material — gut bacterial conversion yields approximately 3–10× less sulforaphane than myrosinase-activated preparation. The patient is almost certainly receiving near-zero sulforaphane from broccoli juice. This is the single most actionable preparation note in this output.

## Beta-Carotene Cross-Flag (Carrot Juice — Patient Taking)

The patient is juicing carrots, which provides beta-carotene. **Food-level carrot juice intake is categorically different from beta-carotene supplementation.** The ATBC and CARET harm signals apply to high-dose beta-carotene supplementation (20–30 mg/day), not to food-level intake from vegetables. Carrot juice at culinary quantities is a V3/V2 cross-vector food (provitamin A → retinoic acid axis); it is not a primary V1 compound. Retain the distinction for the orchestrator: do NOT recommend beta-carotene supplements; do NOT flag carrot juice at culinary doses as harmful on the same basis.

## What I Could Not Establish

1. Tissue concentration data for any of these food compounds in biceps femoris primary or lung metastasis tissue. Plasma PK exists for several compounds; tissue PK in extremity soft tissue is not characterized for dietary polyphenols.

2. Whether the patient's specific juice volumes deliver the same compound concentrations as typical dietary servings (concentration will vary with dilution, juicer efficiency, and produce ripeness).

3. Whether gut microbiome composition at the time of active chemotherapy (post-14 cycles VDC/IE, post-radiation) differs from healthy-volunteer PK studies used to characterize bioavailability figures. Antibiotics co-administered with chemo alter microbiome-dependent polyphenol conversion.

4. Whether fresh-squeezed juice preparation preserves EGCG and apigenin comparably to the tea/herb consumption routes on which bioavailability estimates are based.

5. Direct evidence in CIC-DUX4 cell lines or animal models for any food-level V1 compound — none exists in the published literature.
